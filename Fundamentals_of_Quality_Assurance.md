 ## Definition of Quality Assurance

By carrying out planned activities within an organisation to ensure that goods or processes comply with specified quality criteria, quality Assurance (QA) guarantees high-quality outcomes.
QA plays a significant role in various industries, including manufacturing and healthcare, by ensuring that customers consistently receive finished goods or services that meet their needs.

## Quality control essentials 
* Regarding quality assurance, its importance must be balanced, especially since maintaining a good reputation makes it an essential practice for organisations. Companies that repeatedly provide high-quality products or services through proper QA create trust and credibility among their customer base.
* Repeat business results from satisfied customers who remain loyal due to a company's positive reputation, which prompts them to recommend it by word of mouth
* Conversely, poor-quality goods or services can cause customer dissatisfaction, resulting in negative feedback and damaging the company's image, leading to a difficult recovery.

## Quality Assurances significance 
QA is necessary to reduce potential risks, and reducing the likelihood of costly mistakes is achievable by identifying and solving possible defects early in the process. Implementing comprehensive QA processes enables organisations to detect potential problems early on and rectify them before customer impact occurs, thereby saving precious resources.


## The positive Effects of Quality control 
* Minimising the chances for product recalls and other potential risks through a proactive approach can help prevent severe financial losses or legal problems. QA improves operational efficiencies like: Product enhancements, spotting inefficiencies, and identifies bottlenecks.
* Ongoing evaluation allows for identifying opportunities for corrective action, ultimately leading to streamlined operations with enhanced performance at lower costs.
* Fostering a culture of continuous improvement through quality assurance helps to promote creativity and encourage learning based on previous experiences within an organisation.

## Other benefits of Quality Assurance 
* Ensuring regulatory compliance is critical to its duties, which is the essence of QA. To maintain consumer safety and public health in the healthcare or pharmaceutical industries, it is essential to follow rigorous rules and standards.
* QA procedures enable companies to adhere to regulations by conducting audits and keeping complete records that prove their compliance.
* Not fulfilling regulatory requirements can have serious legal consequences and result in heavy fines or operational shutdowns. This emphasises the need for rigorous QA protocols.
* QA assists in effectively managing risks by analysing all potential issues and creating comprehensive strategies for mitigation.

## Quality Management 
* Focusing on prevention rather than correction makes up the core objective of QA. Preventing defects is at the core of this approach, emphasising comprehensive planning, accurate recordkeeping, structured learning sessions, and steady progress in processes.
* At the beginning stages of a project or product development lifecycle, key stakeholders, such as designers and quality professionals, collaborate to conduct QA activities.
* This includes QA's identification of potential risks and implementation of preventive measures alongside ensuring compliance with quality standards, which minimises errors or failures in the final deliverable. While Quality Assurance proactively identifies potential problems with products or processes before they occur, Quality Control reacts to actual incidents.

## Quality control centre 
* QC focuses on identifying and eliminating product defects before delivering them to customers. At the same time, activities such as sampling and testing are essential in assessing the quality of products or services.
* Detecting and addressing issues is the cornerstone of QC, achieved through corrections, adjustments, and reworks.
* In contrast with QA's broad scope of ensuring high efficacy across all aspects of an organisation's operations, QC hyper-focuses on individual teams' responsibility for analysing product performance according to predetermined standards.
* In determining these criteria for evaluation, one could consider customer requirements or standards.
* Quality control activities are executed during production or service delivery at varying points, including for incoming materials and final inspections before release.

## QA and QC Relationship (qc = quality control)
* There is a diversity of objectives between QA and QC, but they are interrelated. With QC performing the verification, QA's framework and process ensure consistent fulfilment of quality requirements.
* Detection and correction take centre stage in quality control, whereas prevention and improvement are paramount for quality assurance. Both functions must contribute to effectively managing overall quality in an organisation.
* QA and QC need to work together seamlessly for the best possible results. Defining standards and enforcing adherence are crucial components in laying the groundwork for managing quality through QA.
* Through this framework, the QC team is given a set of rules that describe inspection procedures. Feedback and data from QC activities can be used to improve QA processes, leading to better quality results.

## QA developments throughout history 

* Ensuring the quality of products has been present since ancient times, with evidence found in both Egyptian and Mesopotamian cultures' use of codes and inspections. Expert artisans who used high-quality materials and exact measurements built architecture as magnificent as the pyramids in ancient Egypt.
* To guarantee compliance with recognised benchmarks, an inspection was performed, which included a comprehensive assessment of the quality of the products.
* With mass production gaining prominence during the Industrial Revolution in the 18th century, there was a shift in focus from quality management.
* Unique craftsmanship gave way to standardised approaches with an emphasis on monitoring quality.
* Manufacturers have realised how vital it is to examine their products to ensure their reliability, and this marked the early stages of formalising QA.
* Quality Management underwent significant changes during the early twentieth century when Stewart developed statistical quality control techniques. Frederick W. Taylor, known for founding scientific management, was noteworthy during this period.

## Quality procedures and administration 
* To enact better quality control procedures, these pioneers focused on gathering information through data measurement techniques and statistical analyses.
* Incorporated in the mid-twentieth century, Total Quality Management (TQM) was an overall way to handle quality, emphasising engaging all levels of an organisation in continual improvement initiatives.
* After World War II, Deming's principle's impact on Japanese firms' quality practices influenced their commercial achievements.

## Defining P.D.C.A. (Plan-Do-Check-Act)
The P.D.C.A. (Plan-Do-Check-Act) cycle, or the Deming cycle, is a critical component in quality management that must be considered, as this continuous improvement framework helps organisations achieve optimum performance by guiding and enhancing their quality management practices.

## Comprehending the P.D.C.A (Plan-Do-Check-Act) cycle
***Stage 1 - Plan***
* Setting achievable goals and planning accordingly is essential to completing a plan successfully. By identifying areas for improvement, organisations create quality objectives that require specific processes and resource allocations to fulfil. Conducting risk evaluations and studying data is crucial to creating an effective action plan. 
* Meeting quality goals requires careful planning to implement a clear roadmap and strategies during the organisational or project stages.

***Stage 2 - Do***
* We will carry out the previously planned steps during this next phase. Following the outlined processes and procedures is essential for successfully executing this plan.
* In this phase, organisations gather relevant data while implementing their plan by deploying all necessary resources. To maintain consistency and reliability throughout the process, the execution must follow set procedures.

***Stage 3 - Check***
* In the check stage, we assess outcomes against stated goals as part of a process. By collecting data and measuring performance, organisations can evaluate their success in achieving their objectives.
* To succeed at this stage, one needs to prioritise the analysis of data and monitoring KPIs.

***Stage 4 - Act***
* Organisations can spot deviations and improvement opportunities by conducting meticulous checks and evaluations. This stage is called Act. The Act comes after analysing data from previous stages and focusing on implementing solutions. Data analysis enables organisations to find out what is causing problems or inconsistencies and initiate the necessary changes to rectify them.

## The act stage
The primary goal at this stage is to address any identified gaps through practical problem-solving skills. To enhance your overall performance quality, it's vital to document your actions and evaluate their effectiveness.

## P.D.C.A cycles primary objective 
* The P.D.C.A. cycle emphasises continuous iterative improvement; following the completion of the Act stage, it's customary to begin again with a fresh round of planning followed by implementation and evaluation.
* With each new iteration building upon the last one, incremental improvements.

## Applying the P.D.C.A cycle in quality assurance 
Numerous benefits exist when implementing the P.D.C.A. cycle in QA.

* Employing the systematic approach of the P.D.C.A. Cycle can help ensure continuous improvements, and tracking the results of implemented changes through a process helps organisations identify areas for improvement.
* The constant improvement inherent in the cyclical nature of this process allows organisations to enhance their quality management practices and strive for excellence.
* The P.D.C.A. Cycle provides a structure for problem-solving, and by strictly adhering to each stage in the cycle and thoroughly analysing problems to uncover their root causes, organisations can carry out practical corrective actions. Systematic issue addressing enables organisations to avoid their recurrence in the future.
* Quality assurance can be achieved by using the powerful P.D.C.A. Cycle. By adopting a systematic approach using data-driven decision-making and problem-solving techniques, it is possible to achieve ongoing improvements.
* By following a cyclical process for quality management practices, organisations can ensure standardisation while managing risks more effectively.
* With the help of the P.D.C.A. cycle, organisations can modify and perfect their quality performance, leading to increased customer satisfaction rates and greater operational efficiency, resulting in overall organisational prosperity.
